How the rule works

INSS has a cap: in 2026, employee contributions apply to at most R$ 8,475.55 a month. That limit covers the total you earn in the month, not each job separately.

The problem: one employer doesn't know what the other pays. If you didn't report your other jobs, each withholds as if it were the only one. The combined deductions can exceed the cap, and the excess does not raise your pension.

The law allows a refund of what was withheld above the cap in the last five years, with interest at the Selic rate.

Illustrative example

When a review is worth it. And when it isn't.

Usually worth it

  • When your combined INSS earnings were well above the cap for several months.

Usually not worth it

  • When your combined income is close to or below the cap.
  • When one job is a civil service post under its own pension scheme, which doesn't add to INSS.
  • When you are paid only through a company, with no personal deductions.

An important note: with some moderate income combinations, separate withholding may have been lower than required. A careful review considers that before any claim.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as the unauthorized deductions from retirees?

No. That case involves association fees taken from INSS benefits. This is about contributions withheld from working people above the cap.

Does the overpayment increase my pension?

No. Benefits only count up to the cap. That is why the excess can be refunded.

How far back can I claim?

The last five years, counted from each deduction.

I'm a civil servant and also have a private-sector job. Do I qualify?

As a rule, no, because the civil service scheme doesn't add to INSS. The quick check takes this into account.

How do I stop this from happening?

By telling each employer how much you earn and how much has already been withheld elsewhere. The attorney can show you how.
